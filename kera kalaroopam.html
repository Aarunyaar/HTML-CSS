Kerala Kalaroopangal

  1. Kathakali

    2. Kathakali is a classical dance-drama of Kerala, renowned for its vibrant costumes, elaborate makeup, and powerful storytelling. Originating in the 17th century, it blends dance, drama, music, and ritual, with themes drawn mainly from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and other Indian epics. Performers use expressive facial gestures (Navarasas), symbolic hand movements (Mudras), and rhythmic body actions to convey stories, often without spoken dialogue. The colors in makeup represent character types—green for noble heroes, red for villains, and black for demons or hunters—while traditional percussion instruments like the chenda and maddalam enhance the dramatic effect. More than just entertainment, Kathakali reflects Kerala’s cultural richness and spiritual heritage, performed in temples and festivals, and admired worldwide for its grandeur.

  2. Mohiniyattam

    3. Mohiniyattam is a graceful classical dance form of Kerala, traditionally performed by women, and is often called the "dance of the enchantress." Its roots trace back centuries, blending elements of Bharatanatyam and the regional traditions of Kerala. Characterized by gentle swaying movements, elegant footwork, and delicate facial expressions, Mohiniyattam beautifully portrays themes of love and devotion, often dedicated to Lord Vishnu or Lord Krishna. The dance is performed in flowing white or off-white costumes with golden borders, enhancing its charm and simplicity. Accompanied by classical Carnatic music and instruments like the veena, mridangam, and edakka, Mohiniyattam captures the essence of feminine grace and spiritual storytelling, making it one of the most cherished kalaroopangal of Kerala.

  3. Kalaripayattu

    4. Kalaripayattu is one of the oldest martial art forms in the world, originating in Kerala more than 3,000 years ago. Known as the "mother of all martial arts," it combines physical training, combat techniques, self-defense, and even healing practices. The word comes from Kalari (training ground) and Payattu (fight), and training traditionally takes place in a special arena called a Kalari.This martial art includes strikes, kicks, grappling, weaponry (like swords, sticks, and daggers), and advanced techniques of body flexibility and agility. It also emphasizes discipline, mental focus, and respect for the master (Gurukkal). Kalaripayattu is closely linked with Ayurveda and yoga, as it integrates healing practices and physical well-being into its training. Today, it is not only practiced for combat but also performed as a cultural art, showcasing Kerala’s heritage and inspiring modern martial arts and performance techniques worldwide.

  4. Koodiyattam

    5. Koodiyattam is the oldest surviving form of Sanskrit theatre, with its origins in Kerala dating back over 2,000 years. Recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, it is a unique blend of dance, drama, and ritual performed traditionally in temple theatres called Koothambalams. The word "Koodiyattam" means "acting together," as it is performed by both men and women, unlike many other classical art forms.The performances are elaborate, often stretching over several days, and are based on Sanskrit texts as well as ancient stories from epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Actors use highly stylized hand gestures (mudras), facial expressions (rasas), and slow, deliberate body movements to narrate the story, with each scene explored in great depth. The costumes are grand, and the makeup is symbolic, representing different character types. Traditionally, the Chakyar community performed the main roles, while Nangiars (women) played supporting characters, accompanied by percussion instruments like the mizhavu.Koodiyattam is not just theatre but a sacred ritual that blends storytelling, spirituality, and cultural tradition, making it one of the most prestigious kalaroopangal of Kerala.

  5. Ottanthullal

    6. Ottamthullal is a lively and satirical art form of Kerala, created in the 18th century by the poet Kunchan Nambiar. It was developed as a way to make classical stories and social messages more accessible to common people, often with humor and wit. The performance is a solo dance-drama where the artist narrates stories from epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata, while also adding sharp commentary on society, politics, and human behavior.The performer wears a colorful costume with a crown-like headgear and painted face, making the presentation visually striking. Unlike classical forms such as Kathakali, Ottamthullal is more fast-paced, simple, and entertaining, with rhythmic movements and expressions that easily connect with audiences. The accompanying music, sung by the performer or supporting singers, is set to the beat of instruments like the mridangam or edakka.Ottamthullal is admired for its blend of entertainment, satire, and cultural storytelling, making it one of Kerala’s most vibrant kalaroopangal.

  6. Theyyam

    7. Theyyam is a ritualistic performance art of North Kerala, especially popular in the districts of Kannur and Kasaragod. Rooted in ancient tribal traditions, it is both a cultural performance and a form of worship, where performers are believed to embody gods, spirits, or ancestral heroes. The word "Theyyam" comes from "Daivam," meaning god, and during the performance, the artist is revered as a divine presence by the community.The performance is marked by vibrant costumes, towering headgear, elaborate face painting, and energetic dance movements. Each Theyyam represents a particular deity or spirit, and there are over 400 varieties of Theyyam, each with its own story, songs, and rituals. The performances usually take place in temple courtyards or sacred groves (kavus) during festivals, accompanied by traditional percussion instruments like the chenda and elathalam.Theyyam is not only a visual spectacle but also a living tradition that reflects Kerala’s folk beliefs, mythology, and social structure. It serves as a medium through which divine blessings, justice, and protection are believed to reach the people, making it one of the most powerful and spiritual kalaroopangal of Kerala.

  7. Chakyar Koothu

    8. Chakyar Koothu is one of the oldest traditional performance arts of Kerala, performed mainly inside temples as a form of ritual storytelling and entertainment. It is a solo performance, usually by a member of the Chakyar community, where the artist narrates episodes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Puranas in a mixture of Sanskrit and Malayalam.What makes Chakyar Koothu unique is its blend of storytelling, satire, and social commentary. The performer not only narrates mythological tales but also adds witty remarks, humor, and even criticism about contemporary society, politics, or human behavior, making it engaging and thought-provoking for the audience. The performance is accompanied by simple instruments like the mizhavu (a percussion instrument) and ilathalam (cymbals).The performer wears a distinctive headgear (turban-like), ornaments, and facial markings, but the focus remains on facial expressions, gestures, and powerful oration. Chakyar Koothu is considered both a sacred ritual and a cultural art form, preserving Kerala’s oral storytelling tradition with humor, wisdom, and spirituality.
