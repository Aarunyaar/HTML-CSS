Explosion at U.S. Steel’s Clairton Coke Works in Pennsylvania

On August 11, 2025, a massive explosion struck the U.S. Steel Clairton Coke Works plant in Pennsylvania, killing two workers and injuring at least ten others. The blasts occurred around 10:50 a.m. ET, followed by thick black smoke and a fire that quickly spread through parts of the facility. Emergency responders, including 20 EMS units and 7th fire departments, rushed to the site, rescuing one trapped worker and the body of another victim. Five of the injured remain in critical but stable condition, while others have been treated and discharged. The cause of the explosion is still under investigation by U.S. Steel, the U.S. Chemical Safety Board, and local authorities. The incident occurred in batteries 13 and 14 of the plant, which is the largest coke-making facility in North America and plays a critical role in steel production. The Clairton facility has faced past safety and environmental issues, including fines and lawsuits from incidents in 2009, 2010, and 2018. Residents in the surrounding area were briefly advised to stay indoors, though officials reported no hazardous pollution levels. The tragedy comes amid heightened scrutiny following Nippon Steel’s $15 billion acquisition of U.S. Steel, with union leaders and environmental advocates now demanding answers and stronger safety measures.

David Burritt, CEO of U.S. Steel said I end every meeting and every message with the words, ‘Let’s get back to work safely. That commitment has never been more important, and we will honor it.

PennEnvironment’s Executive Director, David Masur, called for justice and scrutiny, stating: “PennEnvironment is sending out our thoughts and prayers to the individuals… We need a full, independent investigation into the causes of this latest catastrophe and a re-evaluation as to whether the Clairton plant is fit to keep operating.”

The Clairton Coke Works is a massive industrial facility along the Monongahela River south of Pittsburgh, and is considered the largest coking operation in North America.
